# Environment replay: literal object inclusion vs an edit IR

`coalton-toplevel` supports separate compilation: a definition compiled in
one unit must rebuild its contribution to the global type environment when
its fasl is loaded, possibly in a later session. Coalton implements this by
recording the environment edits a compilation makes and re-emitting them as
generated Lisp ("replay"). This note characterizes that mechanism, measures
what it emits, and substantiates a proposed redesign: replacing literal
inclusion of compiler-internal objects with an abstracted, versioned IR of
environment edits. The change is also the precondition for the GOAL-025
CLOS conversion and is the concrete form of that goal's "settle the
separate-compilation replay story" step.

## The current mechanism

Three pieces (`src/typechecker/environment.lisp`, `src/entry.lisp`,
`src/utilities.lisp`):

1. **Record.** `define-env-updater` (environment.lisp:243) wraps each
   environment-mutating function so that, when `*update-hook*` is bound, the
   call notifies the hook with the updater's name and its arguments minus
   the environment:
   ```lisp
   (when (boundp '*update-hook*)
     (funcall *update-hook* ',name (cdr args)))
   ```
   `with-environment-updates` (entry.lisp:174) binds the hook to push each
   `(name . args)` onto a log. The recorded args are the live compiler
   objects, unserialized.

2. **Emit.** `make-environment-updater` (entry.lisp:181) turns the log into
   source:
   ```lisp
   `(let ((env *global-environment*))
      ,@(loop :for (fn . args) :in updates
              :collect `(setf env (,fn env ,@(mapcar #'util:runtime-quote args))))
      (setf *global-environment* env)
      (tc:synchronize-type-variable-counter env))
   ```
   `util:runtime-quote` is just `` `',x `` (utilities.lisp:80), so every
   recorded argument is emitted as a literal quoted object.

3. **Reconstruct.** When the emitted source is compiled, each quoted literal
   object is rebuilt through its `make-load-form` method
   (`make-load-form-saving-slots` on every entry struct and its transitive
   slots). Loading the fasl re-runs the `setf env` chain and installs the
   edits into `*global-environment*`.

The only edit with special handling is `set-code`: `make-environment-updater`
de-duplicates repeated `set-code` for the same name via `code-update-eql`
(entry.lisp:200).

## What gets embedded

Of 22 `define-env-updater` forms, 12 carry a compiler-internal object
(the other 10 are `unset-*` / all-symbol edits). The embedded objects span
all three internal representations:

- **Typechecker entries and types** (11 updaters): `set-value-type` (a
  `ty-scheme`), `set-type` (`type-entry`), `set-constructor`
  (`constructor-entry`), `set-class` (`ty-class`), `set-name` (`name-entry`),
  `add-instance` (`ty-class-instance`), `add-specialization`
  (`specialization-entry`), `update-instance-fundeps` (`ty-predicate` +
  list), and the rest -- plus their transitive slots (`ty`, `tgen`, `kind`,
  `qualified-ty`, ...).
- **Parser AST** (1 updater): `set-function-source-parameter-names`
  (environment.lisp:1495) stores a `parser:pattern-list` -- the function's
  source parameter patterns, kept for diagnostics.
- **Codegen AST** (`set-code`, environment.lisp:1598): stores the
  optimizer's codegen `node` for each definition
  (`src/codegen/optimizer.lisp:114,150,188`), so downstream units can inline
  and monomorphize against it (`monomorphize.lisp`, `inliner.lisp`). Its
  parameter is declared `t`, but in practice it is the codegen node tree.

So the replay embeds the parser AST, the typechecker types, and the codegen
AST -- the three representations GOAL-025 is converting to CLOS.

## Measurement

Generated by `entry:codegen` on the benchmark corpus (the replay is the
leading `make-environment-updater` form):

| corpus file | source | total gen | replay | replay % | edits | `#S(` literals |
|-------------|-------:|----------:|-------:|---------:|------:|---------------:|
| 01-small-fact-fib | 474 B | 39 KB | 28 KB | 71% | 14 | 79 |
| 02-medium-classes | 1.6 KB | 314 KB | 277 KB | 88% | 88 | 749 |
| 03-large-inference | 3.1 KB | 721 KB | 660 KB | 92% | 182 | 1906 |

- The replay is the overwhelming majority of generated code, and it grows
  super-linearly: a 6.6x source increase (474 B to 3.1 KB) produces a 24x
  replay increase (28 KB to 660 KB).
- `set-code` dominates the embedded objects: 1714 of 1906 `#S(` literals on
  the large file, 704 of 749 on the medium. `set-value-type` is second
  (full `ty-scheme` / `qualified-ty` / `function-ty` / `tycon` trees).
- Embedded `source:location` slots serialize the source-file path as a
  read-eval form (`#.(make-instance 'source-file :file #A((39) base-char
  . "benchmark/corpus/..."))`), leaking build paths into artifacts.
- Shared sub-objects are emitted with `#1=` / `#1#` circular labels, so the
  emission requires `*print-circle*`.

## Costs of literal inclusion

1. **Representation coupling -- the CLOS-conversion wall.** Because the
   replay embeds internal objects and relies on their readable printing
   (`compile-to-lisp` writes source, then `compile-file`s it), the replay is
   coupled to every embedded struct's layout. Converting any embedded family
   to CLOS breaks it: a CLOS standard-object is not readably printable, so
   `compile-to-lisp` raises `print-not-readable`. This was observed
   converting the parser `pattern` family (it broke `test-compile-to-lisp`),
   and it applies equally to the codegen AST (`set-code`) and the
   typechecker types/environment. So literal inclusion stands in front of
   the parser, codegen, and environment CLOS conversions at once.

2. **Size and super-linear growth.** `make-load-form-saving-slots` emits
   every slot of every node, defaults included, with the codegen AST
   (`set-code`) shipped per definition. The replay is 92% of output on the
   large file and scales far faster than source.

3. **Version fragility.** `make-load-form-saving-slots` ties a fasl to the
   exact slot layout at compile time. A later compiler that changes any
   embedded struct silently produces fasls that reconstruct into the new
   layout with no compatibility check -- there is no version on the
   serialized form.

4. **Determinism / path leakage.** The emission bakes absolute-ish source
   paths via read-eval into the artifact.

## The proposed edit IR

Replace literal object inclusion with an abstract, versioned serialization
of the edit set: the replay becomes data describing *what edits to apply*,
in a representation-independent encoding, rather than a dump of the
in-memory objects.

Shape:

- The edit log is already a clean sequence of `(operation . payload)`
  (`set-value-type`, `add-instance`, `set-code`, ...). Keep that; it is the
  IR's top level.
- Each payload is encoded in a stable schema that does **not** mirror the
  internal struct layout: a `ty-scheme` encodes as a small s-expression of
  type structure (Coalton already prints types via `type-to-string` and
  parses type syntax, so a round-trippable type encoding is largely within
  reach); `name-entry` / `type-entry` / instance entries encode their
  semantically required fields; `source:location` encodes a
  source-identifier plus span, not a reconstructed `source-file` object;
  the `set-code` payload encodes the inlinable definition in whatever form
  the inliner actually needs (see open questions).
- Reconstruction is an explicit decoder per operation that builds the
  current internal objects from the IR. The internal representation is now
  free to change -- only the decoder moves.
- The IR carries a format version; loading checks it and can reject or
  migrate.

This is the separately-compiled-interface model (a serialized, versioned
module signature) rather than a memory-image dump, which is what
`make-load-form` effectively is.

### Why this is the better design (substantiated)

- **It removes the CLOS-conversion wall.** A stable IR boundary means the
  parser, typechecker, and codegen representations can become CLOS (or
  change in any other way) without touching the replay -- only the
  encode/decode at the boundary changes. This is the single largest
  unblock: it converts a cross-cutting coupling into one localized seam.
  The `pattern` 3b case and the codegen `set-code` case both dissolve.
- **It serves redefinability directly.** Recompile-on-redefine and
  redef-detection reason about *which definitions a change affects*. An
  explicit, data-level edit set is exactly that signal -- diffable and
  invalidatable -- whereas an opaque object graph is not. The edit IR and
  the reverse-dependency graph are the same kind of artifact and reinforce
  each other.
- **It makes separate compilation robust.** A versioned schema gives
  cross-compiler-version fasls a compatibility check instead of silent
  layout reinterpretation.
- **It is smaller.** A purpose encoding avoids per-slot defaults, the
  per-location source-file reconstruction, and (for types) the redundant
  shared-node machinery. The current 92%/super-linear emission is strong
  evidence there is substantial room; the exact factor is to be measured
  against a prototype.

### Costs and open questions

- An encoder/decoder must exist for each entry type and the type structure.
  Much of the type side is tractable (Coalton has type printing and type
  syntax parsing); the entries are small records.
- Decode runs at load time. That is compile/load-time cost, not the runtime
  constraint the goal sets aside, but it should be measured.

## Scoping `set-code` (the dominant payload)

`set-code` is the largest payload and the one that embeds the codegen AST,
so it decides how hard the IR is. Reading the producer (optimizer.lisp:
113-114, 150, 188) and every consumer (`tc:lookup-code` in `inliner.lisp`,
`monomorphize.lisp`, `optimizer.lisp:538`, `debug.lisp`) gives a sharper
answer than "compress the node."

What is stored: the *fully optimized*, type-annotated body of each top-level
definition -- a `node-abstraction` for a function, an arbitrary value node
otherwise -- after the entire optimization pipeline. Stored for **every**
binding, unconditionally (`update-binding-env` loops over all bindings). The
nodes are immutable (`:read-only t` slots); no consumer mutates a looked-up
node, all rebuild fresh copies. It is kept because cross-unit inlining and
monomorphization need the source unit's optimized body, and that body is not
re-derivable from the function's type or the already-translated Lisp.

The consumers fall into two classes:

- **Predicate-only** (`monomorphize.lisp:179` candidate validity, the
  `node-abstraction-p` / `inline-p` tests, `update-function-env`): they read
  only *is-abstraction?*, *arity*, *has-keyword-params?*, and the *type* --
  never the body.
- **Body-consuming** (`inliner.lisp:125,364`; `monomorphize.lisp:454,465`;
  `resolve-compount-superclass` at `optimizer.lisp:538`): they splice or
  traverse the full optimized subexpr tree.

The pivotal observation: a definition's *body* is only ever consumed
downstream when it is an **inline target, a monomorphize target, or a
dictionary** reachable by superclass resolution -- a small, explicitly
tracked subset (`inline-p-table`, `monomorphize-table`, instance dictionaries;
heuristic inlining is off by default). Yet `set-code` stores and replays the
full body for *every* definition. So most of the dominant payload is dead
weight in the replay.

This splits the `set-code` IR cleanly:

1. **Common case (not inline / not monomorphize / not dictionary): omit it
   from the replay entirely.** The predicate consumers need only
   is-abstraction?, arity, keyword-params?, and type -- all derivable from
   entries already replayed: `function-env-entry` (arity, inline-p, via
   `set-function`) and the value's `ty-scheme` (type, function-ness,
   keyword args, via `set-value-type`). Confirming this means rewiring the
   handful of predicate consumers to consult those entries instead of
   `lookup-code`; that is the bounded task that validates dropping the
   common-case `set-code` replay. This removes the bulk of the 92% and
   most of the codegen-AST coupling at a stroke.
2. **Residual (inline / monomorphize / dictionary): the body must travel.**
   This is the only case that needs a serialization of the codegen node,
   and it is the focused remaining design -- far smaller surface than "all
   definitions." Options, in order of preference: re-translate from a stored
   *typed* AST (smaller, and the representation the inliner already works in)
   and re-optimize downstream; a compact round-trippable node encoding; or,
   as a fallback, embed the node for just this subset (the coupling then
   survives only for explicitly-inlinable code -- few definitions, opted in).

So the `set-code` payload is mostly *eliminable*, not merely compressible.
That makes the edit IR tractable: the type/entry encodings are
straightforward, and the one hard payload reduces to encoding bodies for the
small inline/monomorphize/dictionary subset.

## Sequencing

This is GOAL-025's "settle the separate-compilation replay story" step, and
it is a precondition for the CLOS conversions of the embedded families: the
parser `pattern` conversion, the codegen `set-code` path, and the
environment redesign all currently break on literal readable emission. The
edit IR should be designed (at least the boundary and the `set-code`
question) before those conversions, not after -- it is what lets them
proceed without each re-deriving an ad hoc serialization.
